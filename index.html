How it works

Same idea as fantasy football. Instead of players, you draft the NFL's 17 officiating crews — each led by a referee who works with the same six officials all season.

1

Draft 3 crews

Six owners, three-round snake draft, 18 picks: all 17 real crews plus the 2012 Replacement Refs, who never get a week off and score the weekly average of every crew that worked.

2

Set your lineup

Start 2 of your 3 crews each week. Each week 1–4 crews get the week off, and an idle crew scores zero — bench it. If you don't have 2 crews with a game, each open spot scores 85% of the weekly average. Your lineup stays put until you change it.

3

Go head-to-head

Your two starters' points face your opponent's two. Every flag in their games counts — either team, accepted, declined or offsetting. The bench doesn't score.

4

Win the Laundry Bowl

Weeks –: play everyone twice. Week 18: 1st vs 2nd for the title, 3rd vs 4th, and 5th vs 6th in the Delicates Bowl.

Scoring

One simple base rate for every flag, then bonuses for the weird stuff.

2 pts per flag
+ 1 pt per 5 yards

The baseline: a 10-yard holding call — the most common flag in football — is worth 4 points. Partial yards count (a 12-yard foul earns 2.4 yardage points).

The rules

  1. Check assignments. Football Zebras posts each week's referee for every game before kickoff. A crew not listed has the week off.
  2. Text the commissioner your 2 starters by .
  3. No text, no change: last week's lineup stands. Week starts the first 2 crews you drafted.
  4. An idle starter scores 0 — like starting a player on bye. The bench never scores.
  5. Open spot: if fewer than 2 of your crews have a game, each spot you can't fill scores 85% of the weekly average (what the Replacement Refs get), once the week is final. Starting an idle crew when you had a working one on the bench still scores 0.
  6. The 2012 Replacement Refs never have a week off. They score the average of every real crew that worked, once the week is final.

Fine print

Where do the numbers come from?

Every flag is read from the official NFL play-by-play (via the free nflverse data project). Officiating crews come from ESPN's game data. Nobody types anything in.

How are tiers set?

What doesn't count?

Preseason and playoff games. Flags that get picked up — the data doesn't record them. Points go to the crew assigned to the game, even if one official is a fill-in that week.

How do standings work?

Win–loss record first; ties broken by total points scored. Week 18 seeds come from the final Week 17 standings. Weekly results lock once official numbers post, so a later lineup change never changes a past result.
