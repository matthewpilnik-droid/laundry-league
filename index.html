How it works

Same idea as fantasy football. Instead of players, you draft the NFL's 17 officiating crews — each led by a referee who works with the same six officials all season.

1

Draft 2 crews

Six owners, two-round snake draft. Twelve crews get drafted; the other five start the season on the waiver wire.

2

Go head-to-head

Each week your two crews' points face your opponent's two. Every flag in their games counts — either team, accepted, declined or offsetting.

3

Work the wire

Each week 1–4 crews get the week off, and an idle crew scores zero. Check the week's assignments and swap in a free agent before Thursday.

4

Win the Laundry Bowl

Weeks 8–17: play everyone twice. Week 18: 1st vs 2nd for the title, 3rd vs 4th, and 5th vs 6th in the Laundry Basket.

Scoring

One simple base rate for every flag, then bonuses for the weird stuff.

2 pts per flag
+ 1 pt per 5 yards

The baseline: a 10-yard holding call — the most common flag in football — is worth 4 points. Partial yards count (a 12-yard foul earns 2.4 yardage points).

Fine print

Where do the numbers come from?

Every flag is read from the official NFL play-by-play (via the free nflverse data project). Officiating crews come from ESPN's game data. Nobody types anything in.

How are tiers set?

What doesn't count?

Preseason and playoff games. Flags that get picked up — the data doesn't record them. Points go to the crew assigned to the game, even if one official is a fill-in that week.

How do standings work?

Win–loss record first; ties broken by total points scored. Week 18 seeds come from the final Week 17 standings. Weekly results lock once official numbers post, so a later trade never changes a past result.
